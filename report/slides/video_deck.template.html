The problem

Post-quantum blockchains have the basic signatures.
They do not have the exotic ones.

Today

Every chain signs with ECDSA, Schnorr or BLS — all elliptic-curve.

Shor breaks all three.

Standardised

NIST has ML-DSA and friends — basic signatures.

A basic signature authenticates a message and nothing more.

Missing

Multi-sig, threshold, adaptor — the schemes that give chains their features.

Post-quantum: mostly paper-only, no working code.

This project turns one paper design — LAS, a lattice adaptor signature — into a tested, benchmarked, blockchain-facing artefact.

The mechanism · four functions added to a plain signature

Publishing a signature leaks a secret — that is what makes a swap atomic

STEP 1
PreSign
σ̂ = (c, ẑ)
Verifies against a statement Y — but is not a spendable signature.
STEP 2
Adapt
z = ẑ + y
Whoever knows the witness y completes it into an ordinary signature.
STEP 3
Publish
σ → chain
On chain it is an ordinary payment. No script, no hash lock, nothing to see.
STEP 4
Extract
y = z − ẑ
The counterparty subtracts and recovers y — which unlocks the other leg.

Two chains, no trusted third party, no on-chain script: both legs settle or neither does.

The negative result that turned over · EVM, real client receipt

“Impossible on chain” was an inference, not a measurement

What I measured first

A complete, validated native verifier: ≈{{gasLasVerifiedM}} M gas — over the EIP-7825 cap. The optimistic variant then moved the cost into a fraud proof that also exceeds it.

What I got wrong

I recorded the cause as structural and inferred that local optimisation could not close the gap. Re-expressing the same predicate closed it.

A real client mined a claim running full verification at {{gasOptReceipt}} gas — {{gasOptCapPct}}% of the cap.

Scope is part of the claim: Simplified Dilithium-III, a 32-byte signed message, that EVM revision. The remaining margin is a message-length budget, and no other parameter set was evaluated.

Negative results · measured, not speculated

Three optimisations, implemented and closed — each names the mechanism that beat it

Compress the statement Y

Truncation is invisible to the adaptor's own functions — they hash whatever statement they are handed — and fatal at ordinary Verify and at Extract, which accepts only on the exact relation.

Deriving Y from a seed compresses it totally and hands over the witness.

Amortise the proof over a batch

Groth16's proof is constant in batch size — so bytes were never the bottleneck to amortise.

The lattice prover's per-swap proof shrinks, but proving and verifying get several times worse.

Optimistic on-chain verification

The honest claim becomes cheap (≈{{gasNaysayClaimM}} M gas), but the worst-case fraud proof costs ≈{{gasNaysayDigestM}} M — over the cap.

A fraud proof that cannot be mined is not a fraud proof.

An untested future-work bullet costs nothing to write and tells a reader nothing. These close two of the three directions outright.

Conclusion

A post-quantum adaptor signature is achievable by reuse.
The bill arrives in bytes.

What the evidence supports

  • All five objectives met; the lattice core reused unchanged, validated by a second independent implementation.
  • Adaptor functionality: at most +{{ovMaxAll}}% of computation at the core boundary.
  • The real cost is communication — z and the statement Y.
  • In a real swap the proof consumes over 98% of the work.

What stays open

  • A hard relation whose statement is small by design — not a better encoding.
  • A proof system succinct at this scale, not only asymptotically.
  • The security of the ML-DSA variant, and of the consensus rule.
  • A live network: for the post-quantum legs the blocker is a consensus rule, not engineering.

No concrete security is formally analysed and nothing settled on a live network — both scoped out by design, and both bound what these numbers mean.
