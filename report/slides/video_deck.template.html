The application first

Two strangers, two chains, no trusted middleman

The adaptor signatures in use on blockchains today are elliptic-curve — which Shor's algorithm breaks. NIST has standardised basic post-quantum signatures; the exotic ones remain unevenly implemented — multi-signatures are actively being built, and that is the gap this project addresses for the adaptor case.

So: can we build one — what does it cost — and will a real chain take it?

Back to the opening questions

Post-quantum atomic swaps are buildable today — the bill arrives in bytes

Can we build one?

Yes — and twice.

Reuse of standardised primitives with zero upstream functions modified, reproduced independently in a second language to a pinned byte-identical digest.

What does it cost?

Almost nothing in computation: at most +{{ovMaxAll}}% for the adaptor layer.

The bill arrives in bytes — and in a whole swap it is the proof, not the signature, that dominates the time.

Will a chain take it?

Ethereum: yes — a real client mined full verification inside one transaction.

Bitcoin: not as it stands — carriage works on a stock node, but verification needs a new consensus rule; I patched one, and settled a whole two-leg swap on it.

Still open, specifically: a relation whose statement is small by construction; a proof system succinct at this scale; and the security of the ML-DSA variant and of the consensus rule.
