## ⚠️ Key facts for THIS project (read first)

1. **poqeth targets HASH-BASED and MULTIVARIATE signatures — NOT lattice.** The four
   schemes it implements/benchmarks in the EVM are **W-OTS⁺, XMSS, SPHINCS⁺** (hash-based)
   and **MAYO** (multivariate-quadratic). 
2. **poqeth EXPLICITLY RULES OUT Dilithium / lattice signatures** — §1.2, p.2 (verbatim):
   > "Thus, signature schemes with large public keys were ruled out from further
   > consideration, e.g., **Dilithium [24]**."
   The reason: Dilithium's large public keys must be read from expensive permanent
   storage, and its arithmetic is costly. So poqeth provides **no** on-chain verifier
   and **no** proof system for Dilithium/ML-DSA/LAS.
3. **poqeth is NOT a zk-proof system.** Its two verification modes are:
   - **(A) On-chain verification** — the smart contract runs the *full, native*
     verification algorithm in Solidity (§3.1, §4, §5).
   - **(B) Naysayer verification** — an **optimistic fraud-proof** mode (§2.3, §3.2):
     the signature is assumed valid unless a "naysayer" submits a short proof `π_nay`
     that verification *fails* at a specific step, within a challenge period. This is
     **NOT** zero-knowledge and **NOT** a SNARK/STARK. It is based on Seres–Glaeser–
     Bonneau "Naysayer proofs" (FC'24) [54].
4. **The zk/STARK route is FUTURE WORK that poqeth did not implement** — §8, "Succinct
   proofs of signature verification" (verbatim, abridged):
   > "A promising approach would be to prove the correct verification of PQ signatures
   > instead of verifying them directly in the EVM … issue a succinct PQ-secure STARK
   > [8] proof … The EVM allows the efficient verification of STARK proofs (≈ 5 million
   > gas) … could be amortized over multiple PQ signatures. **We leave the evaluation
   > and benchmarking of this approach … for future work.**"
5. **Precompile route** is also flagged as external/future (§8): EIP-7592 / EIP-7619
   propose native EVM precompiles for **Falcon**; Allende et al. estimate on-chain
   Falcon verification ≈ **500 million gas**, dropping to ≈ **1200 gas** if a precompile
   is adopted (a protocol-level change poqeth cannot deploy).

**Implication for LAS:** "implement on-chain verification using poqeth's zk-proof" is
not directly realisable — poqeth has no zk-proof and no Dilithium/lattice support. The
faithful poqeth-derived options for LAS are (A) a **native Solidity verifier** of the
(simplified-Dilithium) LAS `Verify`, or (B) a **Naysayer optimistic verifier** for LAS
(a genuine research extension — poqeth explicitly did Naysayer only for hash-based + MAYO,
and lists lattice among future work). The zk-STARK route is a third, much larger effort
that poqeth itself did not attempt.

## Abstract

Explores efficient deployment of standardized PQ digital signatures in the blockchain
environment. Implements and evaluates four PQ signatures in the EVM: **W-OTS⁺, XMSS,
SPHINCS⁺, MAYO**. Focus: optimizing the **gas cost of verification**, since verification
is the *only* signature algorithm executed on-chain (KeyGen/Sign run off-chain and incur
no fees). Two on-chain verification methods are examined; **full on-chain verification is
often prohibitively costly**. **Naysayer proofs (FC'24)** enable a novel *optimistic*
verification mode, which is generally the cheapest — at the cost of additional trust
assumptions. Released as the open-source library `poqeth`.

## 1. Introduction

- Bitcoin settles ≈ 300,000 tx/day; ECDSA/Schnorr sign every tx. No major chain natively
  verifies PQ signatures today.
- Motivation: Shor's threat compels migration from RSA/ECDSA/Schnorr to PQ signatures.

### 1.1 Post-quantum security and cryptocurrencies
- **Bitcoin's PQ verification is considered solved**: its limited script can encode a
  Lamport verification circuit at roughly regular-tx size (Ethan Heilman's script). So
  the paper **focuses on Ethereum** (2nd largest by market cap, largest by tx volume).

### 1.2 The right PQ signature for Ethereum
EVM characteristics that drive scheme choice:
- **Permanent vs transient storage.** SSTORE/SLOAD (permanent) are far costlier than
  arithmetic/hashing. **Signature schemes with large public keys are ruled out — e.g.,
  Dilithium.** Because signatures are only *temporarily* stored in CALLDATA, the paper
  favours **small public keys even at the expense of larger signatures.**
- **Computational efficiency = gas.** Op costs don't track "real" cost: a KECCAK256 (36
  gas) ≈ 5 MULMODs (8 gas). So **hash-based signatures are surprisingly cheap on the EVM.**
- **Signer's state.** Stateful signatures (XMSS) are a *good* fit on a blockchain — the
  verifier contract is a trusted party with transparent state; the signer reads its simple
  counter from the immutable chain.
- **No native floating point.** EVM is a 256-bit-word stack machine with modular integer
  arithmetic only; float libs cost ~600 gas/add (2 orders of magnitude worse). → Falcon
  (float-heavy) is disfavoured.

**Table 1 — EVM opcode gas costs [57]:**

| Opcode | Gas |
|---|---:|
| ADDMOD | 8 |
| MULMOD | 8 |
| KECCAK256 | 36 |
| SSTORE | 20,000 |
| SLOAD | 2,100 |
| MSTORE | 3 |
| MLOAD | 3 |
| CALLDATALOAD | 3 |

Chosen schemes: **W-OTS⁺** [32,34]; **XMSS** [31,33] (stateful, IETF-standardized);
**SPHINCS⁺** [3,10] (stateless, NIST-standardized); **MAYO** [13,14] (multivariate,
compact sigs 321 B at NIST-1, pk 1168 B; NIST additional-signatures round-2 candidate).

### 1.3 Our contributions
1. **On-chain verification** of W-OTS⁺, XMSS, SPHINCS⁺, MAYO in the EVM, with parameter
   choices optimized for gas.
2. **Naysayer verification** — first public implementation of the Naysayer proof paradigm
   for PQ signatures; generally cheapest, cf. Table 2.
3. **Open-source library** poqeth (MIT).

**Table 2 — On-chain vs Naysayer verification gas (best parameters per scheme):**

| Mode | W-OTS⁺ | XMSS | SPHINCS⁺ | MAYO |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On-chain | 222,114 | 4,363,623 | 11,617,690 | 938,752,492 |
| Naysayer | 126,095 | 594,572 | 693,721 | 107,634,064 |

(All Solidity; the EVM natively offers only Keccak-256, SHA-256, RIPEMD-160 hashes.)

**Account abstraction (ERC-4337)** would unify EOAs and contract accounts and let users
authenticate transactions with any signature scheme — the mechanism by which these
"non-native" PQ verifiers would actually be invoked on-chain.

## 2. Preliminaries

### 2.1 Notations
`λ` security parameter; `x ←$ S` uniform sampling; `‖m‖₁` Hamming weight; bold = vectors;
`MT(r) = com_r` Merkle root; `MT.Vrfy(com_r, i, rᵢ, π_{r,rᵢ}) → {0,1}` membership proof
(authentication path). Similar notation for XMSS/L-tree vector commitments.

### 2.2 The Ethereum Virtual Machine
Quasi-Turing-complete stack machine, 1024 stack depth, 256-bit words. `block_gas_limit`
(currently 30M) upper-bounds per-block computation but is protocol-adjustable. Solidity /
Vyper / Yul compile to EVM bytecode; poqeth implements in **Solidity**. EOAs vs contract
accounts; every valid tx currently needs an ECDSA signature. EVM natively supports only
Keccak-256, SHA-256, RIPEMD-160.

### 2.3 Naysayer proofs (the core idea)
A **Naysayer proof system** `Π_nay` for a proof system `Π = (Setup, Prove, Verify)` and NP
relation `R` lets one **efficiently prove that a given proof `π` is INCORRECT**. Useful
when `π` can be checked much more cheaply for *incorrectness* than for correctness.

**Definition 2.1 (Naysayer proof).** Tuple `(NSetup, NProve, Naysay, VerifyNay)`:
- `NSetup(1^λ, 1^{λ_nay}) → (crs, crs_nay)`.
- `NProve(crs, x, w) → π`: compute `π' ← Π.Prove(crs,x,w)` and `com` a commitment to the
  evaluation trace of `Π.Verify(crs,x,π')`; output `π := (com, π')`.
- `Naysay(crs_nay, (x,π), Aux_nay) → π_nay`: given `x`, `π=(com,π')` (π' possibly invalid)
  and auxiliary info, output a naysayer proof `π_nay` disputing `π'`.
- `VerifyNay(crs_nay, (x, π), π_nay) → {0,1,⊥}`: output whether evidence against `π` is
  sufficient to reject (0) or inconclusive (⊥).

Must satisfy **correctness** (any incorrect `π` can be naysayed) and **soundness** (a
*correct* `π` cannot be naysayed except with negligible probability). Formal defs in
Appendix A; per-scheme security proofs in Appendix B.

## 3. System model

Key generation and signing run **off-chain** in both modes.

### 3.1 On-chain verification
A smart contract runs the **full** PQ verification in the EVM (ERC-4337-style standardized
interface). Assumes only EUF-CMA of the scheme. The signature lives in CALLDATA during the
tx and is discarded afterwards. **Expensive on-chain verification motivates the optimistic
(Naysayer) mode.**

### 3.2 Naysayer signature verification (optimistic)
Two steps:
- **Store signature on-chain.** The contract does **not** verify; it optimistically
  accepts. In practice, only the signature's **Merkle commitment (32 bytes)** is stored in
  the contract (deletable after the challenge period); the full signature sits in a **data
  availability layer**, readable by everyone for naysaying. The Merkle commitment of the
  signature must be *computed* by the contract (else a malicious signer could give a faulty
  commitment that can't be efficiently naysayed).
- **Challenge period** (e.g., 1 hour). Anyone may challenge by sending `π_nay` pointing to
  the failing step. If a valid `π_nay` is verified, the signature is deemed **incorrect**.
  If **no** valid `π_nay` arrives before the deadline, the signature is **accepted**.

**Added trust assumptions (vs on-chain mode):** synchronous network (else `π_nay` may
arrive late while an adversary delays messages); **existential honesty** (≥1 honest party
monitors and naysays when needed); censorship-resistant chain. Naysayer mode is generally
more gas-efficient than on-chain verification (Sections 4–5).

*Resorting to full on-chain verification:* the modes are not mutually exclusive; an app
could use on-chain verification even when no `π_nay` is expected — sensible when signatures
are faulty more often than not (a cost/game-theoretic tradeoff left to future work).

## 4. Hash-based signature scheme verification in the EVM

All schemes implemented with **Keccak-256** (cheapest EVM hash; slight deviation from
NIST/IETF standards, which mandate SHA-256/SHAKE, for gas efficiency — every hash op is
≥1 order of magnitude cheaper with Keccak).

### 4.1 W-OTS⁺
Chaining function `c_k^i(x,r) = h_k(c_k^{i-1}(x,r) ⊕ rᵢ)`, `c_k^0(x,r)=x`. KeyGen→(sk,pk,r,k);
Sign hashes each base-`w` word `bᵢ` of `M‖C` (C = checksum) `bᵢ` times; **Vrfy** finishes
each chain to `w-1` and compares to `pk`. Message length `m=256` bits; `l=l₁+l₂` chains.

**Table 3 — W-OTS⁺ pk-size vs hashing tradeoff** (m=256):

| w (=l₁+l₂) | 4 | 8 | 16 | 256 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| l | 136 | 90 | 67 | 34 |
| pk (bytes) | 4352 | 2880 | 2144 | 1088 |
| #Hash in Verify (avg) | 272 | 360 | 536 | 4352 |

**On-chain finding:** hashing is the bottleneck, so verification gas grows monotonically
with `w`; **w=4 is optimal** (most messages have Hamming weight ≈100–150; `E[‖H(M)‖₁]=128`).
pk is sent via cheap CALLDATA, so large pk doesn't hurt on-chain cost. At w=4, ‖H(M)‖₁=128:
**295,309 gas**. (Table 4 gives weighted averages; **222,114 gas** at w=4.)

**Naysayer (§4.1.1):** signer stores `com_{σ,pk} = MT(σ,pk,M)`. If a chain index `i` is
faulty, `π_nay` opens `com` at the four items `(σᵢ, pk_i, r, bᵢ)` with Merkle paths; the
verifier checks Eq. (3) for the *single* index `i`. **w=16 is optimal for Naysayer**
(smaller sigs/pk → shorter Merkle paths; shaves 21.46% at w=4, but the single-chain
finish makes w=16 best overall).

**Table 4 — W-OTS⁺ weighted verification gas:**

| Mode | w=4 | w=8 | w=16 | w=256 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On-chain | 222,114 | 223,478 | 272,355 | 1,971,090 |
| Naysayer | 174,448 | 139,821 | 126,095 | 237,908 |

### 4.2 XMSS
Merkle tree `XT` of `2^h` W-OTS⁺ leaves with bitmasks; node
`n_{i,j} = H(n_{2i,j-1} ⊕ b_{l,j} ‖ n_{2i+1,j-1} ⊕ b_{r,j})`. Verify = check the W-OTS⁺
signature, recompute `pk_{W_i}` via L-tree, then check the authentication path (Eq. 6).
Parameter sets `(w,h) ∈ {4,16}×{4,8,16,20}`.

**On-chain findings:** **w=4 optimal**; most efficient set `(w,h)=(4,4)` → **4,363,623 gas**
(averaged over Hamming weights). Tree height `h` matters less than message Hamming weight;
gas is ~linear in ‖H(M)‖₁ (e.g. `7,874,574 − 37,585x` for (w,h)=(4,8)).

**Naysayer (§4.2.1):** three failure causes — faulty W-OTS⁺ chain (as §4.1.1), faulty
L-tree computation, or faulty XMSS auth path. Naysaying an auth path opens the commitment
at the failing hash + sibling nodes (Fig. 4, Eq. 7). **Table 5** breaks down the three
Naysayer types; XMSS Naysayer ≥ 2.67× less efficient than W-OTS⁺ on-chain but far cheaper
than XMSS on-chain (best ≈ **594,572 gas**).

### 4.3 SPHINCS⁺
Stateless; hypertree of `d` layers of XMSS-like trees (Fig. 5) whose W-OTS⁺ keys sign the
roots below, terminating in a **FORS** few-time signature of the message. Bit length
`m = ⌊(k·a+7)/8⌋ + ⌊(h−⌊h/d⌋+7)/8⌋ + ⌊(⌊h/d⌋+7)/8⌋` (Eq. 8).

**On-chain findings:** parameter `d` (# hypertree layers) dominates gas (Fig. 7). NIST-1
gas-optimal set `(h,d,a,k)=(63,10,12,15)`: **11,617,690 gas** (w=4), 13,376,297 (w=16).

**Naysayer:** five fault types (hash at an intermediate node; W-OTS⁺ or FORS faulty;
their hash-commit variants). **Table 6** breaks these down; best Naysayer ≈ **693,721 gas**.

## 5. MAYO — a multivariate-quadratic signature in the EVM

MAYO (Beullens): whip a small oil space `O` (dim `o`) into a larger MQ system `P*` with a
trapdoor. Verify checks `P*(u) =? H(M‖s)` (an MQ evaluation over `𝔽_q`).

**Two key EVM implementation decisions:**
- **Field `𝔽_q`:** EVM can't do non-prime-cardinality Galois fields natively; implementing
  `𝔽_{16}=GF(2⁴)` multiplication needs a stored mult table (costly). So poqeth uses
  **q=31** (prime) over q=16, trading some MQ efficiency for cheaper EVM arithmetic.
- **PRG:** replaced AES-128-CTR (not native) with **Keccak-256** to whip up the public key.

**Parameter set `(q,m,n,o,k)=(31,60,62,6,10)` (NIST-1, leakage-free variant):**
single MAYO verify = **938,752,492 gas** (≈ 938.75 M — the most expensive on-chain scheme,
because the ≈115 KB expanded public-key map is NOT stored (recomputed from seed every
verification), and linear ops (polynomial evaluation) take **84% of the gas**. Could be sped
up with SIMD-style batching — future work.

**Naysayer (§5):** `P*(u)=?H(M‖s)` fails if ≥1 of the `m` MQ equations `pᵢ(u)` is wrong.
Signer commits `v := (pk, σ, M)`, stores `com_v := MT(v)`; naysayer opens `com_v` at the
single faulty equation `i` (message, `s`, `u`, coefficients of `pᵢ`) and checks
`pᵢ(u) =? tᵢ` (the i-th word of `H(M‖s)`). MAYO Naysayer = **107,634,064 gas** — a major
improvement over on-chain MAYO but still the priciest of the four (large MQ system).

## 6. Naysayer proofs for more NIST PQ candidates (high-level, not implemented)

Systematically sketches Naysayer proof systems for NIST second-round *additional* signature
candidates, "to provide high-level ideas … We leave the implementation and evaluation …
to future work."
- **§6.1 Code-based:** LESS (code equivalence; naysay a bad reduced-row-echelon-form / a
  non-monomial matrix), CROSS (restricted syndrome decoding; naysay the bad iteration;
  38-byte pk, well-suited to Ethereum). Expected to admit the biggest Naysayer speedups.
- **App. C.1 Multivariate:** UOV (simpler verify than MAYO but larger pk → unlikely for
  Ethereum).
- **App. C.2 Isogeny/lattice-iso:** SQIsign (small pk/sigs; costly verify but Naysayer-
  friendly by writing the challenge isogeny as a chain of 2-isogenies), Hawk.
- **App. C.3 MPC-in-the-head** (MQOM, Mirath, SDitH, Ryde, FAEST, PERK): keep the MPC party
  count `n` small (2–3) so a faulty instance runs a low-party MPC to naysay.

## 7. Related work

- Stewart et al. [55], improved in [35]: slow **commit-delay-reveal** UTXO transition to PQ
  (spend a pre-quantum UTXO, reveal a previously-committed PQ pk + PQ sig).
- Fawkescoin [17] (symmetric-only PQ chain); Chaum et al. [20] (ECDSA-compatible fallback
  deriving sk from W-OTS⁺); Giechaskiel et al. [30] (Bitcoin under broken primitives).
- **Account abstraction (ERC-4337)** [19]: the enabling mechanism for on-chain custom PQ
  verifiers.
- **No prior systematic study** benchmarks (standardized) PQ signature *verification* gas in
  the EVM — poqeth fills this gap. Only prior efforts: **EIP-7592, EIP-7619** and Allende et
  al. [2] propose a **precompile** to verify **Falcon**; on-chain Falcon ≈ **500 M gas**,
  → ≈ **1200 gas** with the precompile (cheaper than ECDSA). But Falcon (floats) is worse-
  suited to the EVM than the chosen schemes.

## 8. Conclusion and future directions

Full on-chain PQ verification is **moderately-to-prohibitively costly** on Ethereum mainnet
(Table 7). Open directions:
- **Precompile contracts for PQ verification** (à la EIP-7592/7619 for Falcon). Open design
  question: full-signature precompiles vs precompiles for specific bottleneck subroutines
  (Merkle path verification, polynomial/MQ evaluation).
- **Succinct proofs of signature verification (zk/STARK)** — *future work, not done here*:
  a powerful prover proves correct verification of many PQ signatures off-chain and posts a
  **succinct STARK proof**; the EVM verifies a STARK for **≈ 5 M gas**, amortizable over
  many signatures. "We leave the evaluation and benchmarking of this approach … for future
  work." (This is the closest thing in the paper to what is loosely called "the zk-proof
  approach" — but poqeth **did not implement it**, and did not target lattice/Dilithium.)
- **Benchmark more PQ schemes** (esp. CROSS, MPC-in-the-head — biggest Naysayer speedups).
- **Naysayer prover/verifier tradeoffs**: committing to the whole EVM-word-level trace →
  Naysayer verifier need only re-execute the incorrectness of a *single EVM opcode*, at the
  cost of larger signer commitment time. Future work.

**Table 7 — On-chain vs Naysayer verification USD cost** (Oct 27 2024; 1 ETH = 2489 USD,
median gas 5.08 Gwei):

| Mode | W-OTS⁺ | XMSS | SPHINCS⁺ | MAYO |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On-chain | $2.76 | $54.31 | $144.58 | $12,411.01 |
| Naysayer | $1.57 | $7.40 | $8.63 | $1,339.48 |

## Relationship to LAS / this project (analysis, not from the paper)

- LAS's on-chain settlement publishes an **adapted signature that is an ordinary
  (simplified-Dilithium) signature**. To verify it on-chain we need a lattice verifier —
  exactly the case poqeth **excluded**. So poqeth gives us **methodology** (native on-chain
  vs optimistic Naysayer; the gas-tradeoff framing; the "verification is the only on-chain
  cost" argument) but **no reusable lattice verifier**.
- Our existing `evm/LASVerifyCost.sol` estimate (≈16.7 M gas for native simplified-Dilithium
  verify at D3) sits between poqeth's XMSS (4.36 M) and SPHINCS⁺ (11.6 M) / MAYO (938 M)
  on-chain figures — i.e., a lattice on-chain verifier is *feasible within a block* but
  expensive, consistent with poqeth's "moderately-to-prohibitively costly" headline.
- **Faithful poqeth-derived paths for LAS on-chain verification:**
  1. **Native Solidity verifier** (poqeth *on-chain mode* applied to lattice) — complete,
     no simplification, deterministic, ~16.7 M gas. Most tractable.
  2. **Naysayer optimistic verifier** for LAS (poqeth's headline mode; a genuine extension
     to lattice, which poqeth left as future work) — cheaper but a research contribution
     (must design a naysayer proof isolating the single failing NTT/pointwise/norm step).
  3. **zk-STARK of LAS Verify** (poqeth's §8 future work) — smallest on-chain footprint
     (~5 M gas, amortizable) but the largest engineering effort; needs an external proving
     stack; poqeth did not build it.
